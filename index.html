The week

Three sessions · three mobility days · one rest

Three strength sessions a week is where beginners actually gain — muscle and connective tissue rebuild on the off days, not during the work. The other four days still get a checkbox so the habit never breaks.

Your kit

Everything else needs a floor and a doorway

The whole program runs on a floor, a doorway, a sturdy table and two chairs. Switch a piece of kit on and the rungs that need it appear in the ladders; leave it off and they stay hidden, so nothing in your program is something you cannot actually do.

Progression ladders

Your current rung is marked

You do not add weight in calisthenics — you change leverage. When you can hit the top of the rep range on every set, for two sessions running, step up a rung and let the reps drop again.

    Form rules that prevent most injuries

    • Full range beats extra reps. Chest to the floor, chin over the bar, hip crease below the knee. A half rep trains a half body.
    • Leave two in the tank. Stop each set when two clean reps remain. Grinding to failure every set wrecks the next session.
    • Slow the way down. Two to three seconds lowering, one second up. The lowering half is where most of the strength is built.
    • Ribs down, glutes on. In push-ups, planks and hangs, brace as if bracing for a punch. A sagging low back is the most common beginner fault.
    • Warm the wrists and shoulders. Sixty seconds each before pushing days. Wrist and elbow pain is what ends most beginner runs, not muscle soreness.
    • Sharp pain is a stop sign. Soreness spread over a muscle for a day or two is training. Pinpoint pain in a joint means back down a rung.
